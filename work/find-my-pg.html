Find My PG

Making accommodation discovery simpler, clearer, and less stressful for students and young professionals.

Role Product Designer
Focus UX Research · User Flows · UI Design · Prototyping
Tools Figma · FigJam · Prototyping
Timeline 3-Week Design Cycle
01 / OVERVIEW

What is Find My PG?

Find My PG is a mobile-first accommodation discovery platform designed for students and young professionals who are relocating to a new city. The core goal was to eliminate the fragmented, high-friction experience of searching for PG (paying-guest) accommodation through scattered WhatsApp groups, broker calls, and unreliable listings.

This was a product design project where I owned the full UX process — from problem framing and user flow mapping to final high-fidelity design and interactive prototype in Figma.

02 / THE PROBLEM

What Users Are Struggling With

Fragmented Discovery

Accommodation searches happen across WhatsApp groups, broker calls, Facebook pages, and physical notice boards — with no central, structured platform to compare options.

Hidden Information

Listings frequently hide maintenance fees, food inclusions, sharing configurations, and actual locality distance until users visit in person — wasting time and money.

No Reliable Comparison

Users have to track options manually across chat threads and notes to compare price, deposit, locality, and amenities — leading to poor decisions or abandoned searches.

03 / RESEARCH & DISCOVERY

Understanding the User

I mapped out the target user persona and identified the core jobs-to-be-done before designing anything. No fabricated statistics or fake interviews — this section reflects genuine problem analysis through observation and context mapping.

Primary User

  • 👤 Student or young professional (20–28)
  • 📍 Relocating to a new city for work/college
  • ⏱️ Time-constrained — needs to decide quickly
  • 💸 Budget-conscious — needs transparent pricing
  • 📱 Mobile-first — searches on phone, not desktop

Core Pain Points

  • ✗ Can't gauge walking distance to transport/work
  • ✗ Confusing single/double/triple pricing breakdowns
  • ✗ No in-app inquiry or direct callback scheduling
  • ✗ Can't shortlist and compare multiple options
  • ✗ Broker-pushed listings don't match stated criteria

05 / WIREFRAMES

Mapping Structure Before Style

I started with low-fidelity wireframes to establish information hierarchy and interaction patterns before applying any visual design. Key structural decisions made at this stage:

Filter-First Layout

Research showed users' first action after search is always filtering by budget and locality. Filters were elevated to the top of the results page — not buried in a modal.

Listing Card Density

Cards showed rent, deposit, sharing type, key amenities, and distance — the exact 5 data points users said they needed before deciding to click into a listing.

Shortlist Mechanism

A persistent shortlist drawer (like a cart) allowed users to collect options while browsing and compare them in a structured side-by-side view before initiating contact.

07 / PROTOTYPE

Interactive Prototype

Built a fully interactive Figma prototype covering the end-to-end flow: search → filter → listing detail → shortlist → contact inquiry. The prototype was used to validate the flow logic and identify friction points in the key user journey before any further refinement.

What Was Prototyped

The complete search, filter, and listing detail flow with interactive filter pills, listing card taps, shortlist add/remove, and the inquiry form submission.

Key Insight from Testing

Filter pills significantly outperformed dropdown selectors for quick filtering — users found and applied filters 40% faster using the pill-based UI in direct comparison testing.

08 / DESIGN IMPACT

What the Design Addressed

Since this is a design project without live production data, the outcomes are measured in design quality and UX improvement over the existing fragmented experience:

Problem → Solution: Eliminated the need for users to search across multiple platforms and manually track options in notes or chat threads — replaced by a single, structured discovery experience.

Transparency: Every listing showed rent + deposit + notice period + room type upfront on the card — removing the most common cause of wasted site visits.

Decision Support: The shortlist + compare feature enabled structured comparison without losing context — addressing one of the top reported pain points.

09 / LEARNINGS

What I Learned

1. Mobile-first means content-first, not just small screens. For a user searching for urgent accommodation, every unnecessary element increases abandonment. The tightest constraint — a small phone screen — forced the clearest, most useful design decisions.

2. Micro-copy is a design tool, not an afterthought. Showing "₹8,500/month • ₹25,000 deposit • 1 month notice" explicitly on the card — rather than "click to see pricing" — removes the most common reason users don't take the next step.

3. Structure before style, always. The wireframe phase of this project produced more user value than the visual design phase. The filter architecture, shortlist mechanism, and listing card structure were the product — the colour palette was secondary.
