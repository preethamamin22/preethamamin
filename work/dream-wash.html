PROJECT 02 UX/UI Design · Mobile Experience · Service Design

Dream Wash

A simpler digital experience for on-demand laundry — from 3-tap booking to doorstep delivery.

Role Product Designer
Focus UX/UI · Mobile Design · Service Journey · Prototype
Tools Figma · Mobile Components · User Journey Maps
Timeline 2-Week Concept & Design
01 / OVERVIEW

What is Dream Wash?

Dream Wash is an on-demand laundry service app designed to simplify the entire laundry experience — from booking pickup to tracking garment status and receiving clean, folded clothes at your door. This was a concept product design project focused on eliminating friction from a service that most people find annoying and time-consuming.

02 / THE PROBLEM

What Makes Laundry Services Frustrating

On-demand laundry services exist — but the digital experience for booking and tracking them is often worse than the physical alternative. The key friction points:

Manual Booking

Most laundry pickups require a phone call, WhatsApp message, or showing up in person — with no standard pickup window or confirmation system.

Zero Visibility

Once clothes are handed over, users have no idea whether they're being washed, dried, or waiting to be picked up — leading to anxiety and unnecessary follow-up calls.

Confusing Pricing

Pricing by individual garment type creates decision fatigue and invoice surprises. Users want to estimate cost before booking — not after receiving the bill.

03 / RESEARCH & DISCOVERY

Understanding the User Context

Primary User

  • 👤 Working professional or student
  • 🏠 Lives in a hostel, PG, or small apartment
  • ⏱️ Time-poor — laundry is a chore, not a priority
  • 📱 Used to on-demand apps (Swiggy, Uber, Zepto)
  • 💰 Wants predictable, transparent pricing

Key Jobs-to-be-Done

  • ✦ Book a pickup in under 60 seconds
  • ✦ Know exactly when clothes will be ready
  • ✦ See live status without calling anyone
  • ✦ Estimate cost before committing to an order
  • ✦ Receive clothes at a convenient delivery time
04 / USER FLOW

The 4-Step Service Journey

Reduced the entire laundry service lifecycle to four clear, sequential steps — matching how users think about the task, not how the service operates internally:

01 Select Bag Standard, Large, or XL
→
02 Schedule Pickup Date & time window
→
03 Live Tracking Step-by-step status
→
04 Delivery Clean & folded
05 / WIREFRAMES

Structural Decisions Before Visual Design

Bag Capacity Visualizer

Replaced confusing itemized garment lists with simple bag size icons (Standard ~15 items, Large ~25 items, XL ~40 items) — making pricing estimation immediate and visual.

Pickup Time Slots

Designed as a horizontal scroll of 2-hour windows rather than a date picker — matching how users think about "morning," "afternoon," and "evening" rather than specific timestamps.

Progress Stepper

A persistent vertical milestone tracker showing: Picked Up → Washing → Drying → Folding → Out for Delivery. Users could check status at a glance without opening a detail screen.

06 / UI DESIGN

Clean, Confident Visual Design

The visual direction was intentionally clean and bright — a contrast to the typical dark/muted tone of logistics apps. The goal was to make laundry feel effortless and pleasant, not like a chore you're managing through a tool:

Colour Palette

#2563EB #3B82F6 #1E293B #22C55E

Blue primary creates trust and cleanliness associations. Green confirms successful actions. Dark slate for text — high contrast on white backgrounds.

Status Indicators

Picked Up ✓ In Washing 🧼 Drying 💨 Out for Delivery 📦 Delivered 🎉
07 / PROTOTYPE

Interactive Flow Validation

Built a fully interactive prototype in Figma covering the complete booking flow: bag selection → time slot selection → order confirmation → live status tracker → delivery confirmation. The prototype also included the empty/error states to validate real-world edge cases.

What Was Validated

The bag selection mental model (size vs. item count), the pickup time slot UI, and the progress tracker visibility — all tested against the core user journey before finalising the UI.

Design Decision Confirmed

Pre-packaged bag choices significantly outperformed itemized garment counts for simplicity. Users made faster decisions and reported less "thinking required" with the bag model.

08 / DESIGN IMPACT

What the Design Addressed

Booking Simplicity: Reduced the mental model from "calculate garments, call a number, negotiate pickup time" to a 3-tap selection flow with a fixed time slot — eliminating every decision the user didn't need to make.

Anxiety Reduction: The live progress tracker directly addresses the biggest emotional pain point — "where are my clothes?" — without requiring any interaction from the user.

Pricing Clarity: Bag-based pricing (e.g., Standard bag = ₹199) replaces per-item ambiguity — giving users a cost estimate before they commit to an order.

09 / LEARNINGS

What I Learned

1. Reducing decision fatigue is a design superpower. The best UX decisions on this project were about removing choices, not adding features. Bag sizes instead of garment counts. Time windows instead of exact timestamps. Every removal made the product feel simpler and smarter.

2. Status visibility reduces anxiety better than speed. Users don't necessarily need their laundry done faster — they need to know it's happening. A clear progress tracker addresses the emotional job-to-be-done better than any speed improvement.

3. Service design is product design. The offline service steps (pickup, washing, delivery) directly shaped the app's information architecture. Designing the digital layer without understanding the service layer would have produced a product that worked in theory but not in practice.
