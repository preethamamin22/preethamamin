01 / OVERVIEW

What is Outvox Solution?

Outvox Solution is a corporate services platform for B2B clients. The product needed to communicate service offerings clearly, establish credibility, and convert website visitors into business inquiries. It required a modern, professional digital presence that could work as both a marketing asset and a functional platform.

I took full ownership of designing and building this platform — from the first conversation about requirements to the final deployed product.

02 / THE PROBLEM

What Needed to Be Solved

No Digital Presence

Operating without a professional web platform made it difficult to establish trust with B2B clients before a conversation even began.

Unclear Service Communication

Potential clients couldn't clearly understand what services were offered, their scope, or how to initiate contact — leading to drop-off.

No Conversion Path

There was no structured user journey from landing to inquiry. The platform needed clear CTAs and a logical service-to-contact flow.

03 / PRODUCT THINKING

Requirements, Priorities & Architecture

Before opening Figma, I mapped out what the platform needed to accomplish at each stage of the visitor journey. The key question was: what does a B2B client need to see in the first 10 seconds to feel confident reaching out?

Core Requirements

  • ✦ Clear hero with service positioning
  • ✦ Structured service overview section
  • ✦ Credibility signals (about, approach)
  • ✦ Direct, low-friction contact path
  • ✦ Mobile-responsive across all devices
  • ✦ Fast load time and clean performance

Information Architecture

  • 01 Hero — value prop + primary CTA
  • 02 Services — what we offer
  • 03 About — who we are, why trust us
  • 04 Process — how we work
  • 05 Contact — inquiry form + direct channels

05 / WIREFRAMES

Early Design Thinking

I started with low-fidelity layouts in Figma to establish the right structural hierarchy before applying any visual design. Key decisions made at this stage:

Above the Fold Priority

The hero needed to communicate the company's purpose and primary CTA within a single viewport — no scrolling required to understand what the business offers.

Service Card Structure

Services were structured as scannable cards rather than long paragraphs — reducing cognitive load and making it easy for B2B clients to identify relevant offerings quickly.

Contact Accessibility

Placed a secondary contact CTA within every major section — ensuring visitors could initiate contact at any point in the journey without needing to scroll to the bottom.

08 / VIBE CODING

AI-Assisted Development in Practice

I used AI-assisted development to translate the designed experience into a functional interface and iterate quickly between design decisions and live implementation. This is what "vibe coding" means in practice — not just writing prompts, but designing with implementation awareness.

⚡ What This Looked Like
  • ✦ Exported Figma design tokens (colors, spacing, type scales) directly into CSS custom properties — zero manual guesswork.
  • ✦ Used Cursor + LLM prompting to generate React layout blocks, then refined them against the Figma spec in real time.
  • ✦ Iterated between the Figma file and the working browser in tight loops — catching spacing issues, mobile breakpoints, and interaction states before deployment.
  • ✦ Deployed to Vercel with continuous updates as design decisions evolved — no separate "dev sprint" required.

10 / LEARNINGS

What I Learned

1. Owning the full stack changes how you design. When you know you're building it, you design with implementation constraints in mind from the start — fewer "this will be hard to code" moments, and more confident design decisions.

2. Zero handoff loss is the real benefit. When the designer builds the code, the spacing, typography, and micro-interactions match the original Figma vision exactly — no engineering compromises, no spec translation errors.

3. Design and iteration happen in the same loop. User feedback from real clients was implemented from design update to live code in hours, not sprint cycles. This is the real advantage of the designer-as-developer workflow.
